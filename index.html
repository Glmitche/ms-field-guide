Interchange tables
How to read a rate schedule and calculate client dollar impact — the skill Andrew said even experienced RMs find challenging
Andrew said: "Twice a year Visa and Mastercard release updates — interchange increases, criteria changes to fees. Every year it's different and we're all picking up the pieces." Reading these tables and translating them into client dollar impact is a core RM skill.
How interchange rates work
Interchange rates are expressed as a percentage + flat fee per transaction. The rate that applies depends on: (1) card type, (2) how the transaction was processed, and (3) what data was submitted with it.
Formula: Interchange cost = (Transaction amount × rate%) + flat fee per transaction

Example: A $500 CNP transaction at 1.80% + $0.10 = ($500 × 0.018) + $0.10 = $9.00 + $0.10 = $9.10 interchange cost

Payment Explorer
The monthly analytics tool Andrew uses to proactively identify opportunities and issues for every client
Andrew said: "I go into Payment Explorer once a month. I look at authorization rates — can they be better? Fees they're getting that are avoidable? If I see something I always reach out to my clients proactively. That's how you show value."
What you're looking for — the 5 key metrics
Authorization rate
Percentage of attempted transactions approved. Benchmark: industry average is typically 85-90% for CNP, higher for CP. A drop of 1-2% month-over-month is a red flag worth a client call.
→ If below benchmark or trending down: "I was reviewing your account and noticed your auth rate dropped from 87% to 85% last month. Let's talk about what might be driving that and what we can do."
Decline rate breakdown
Hard declines (do not retry — invalid card, stolen) vs. soft declines (retry is possible — insufficient funds, temporary hold, velocity limit). High soft decline rates often indicate retry logic issues or authorization strategy problems.
→ If soft declines are high: coordinate with technical team about retry optimization. A good retry strategy can recover 20-40% of soft-declined transactions.
Avoidable fees
Fees the client is paying that could be reduced or eliminated through data submission changes or processing adjustments. Level 2/3 data gaps, misrouted transactions, and incorrect MCC codes are common culprits.
→ Quantify before you call. "This fee pattern is costing you approximately $X/month — here's what's causing it and how we fix it."
Chargeback ratio trend
Percentage of transactions resulting in chargebacks, tracked month over month. Visa's threshold is 1.0%; exceeding it triggers the Visa Dispute Monitoring Program (VDMP). Early warning is critical.
→ If trending toward 0.7-0.8%: alert the client now, before they breach. "You're at 0.75% and the network threshold is 1%. Let's get ahead of this."
Volume and mix trends
Changes in overall processing volume, card type mix (credit vs. debit, consumer vs. commercial), and CP vs. CNP split. Shifts in mix affect pricing — a client moving more volume to CNP rewards cards will see cost increases.
→ Use volume trends to frame the revenue opportunity conversation: "Your volume is up 12% YoY — let's talk about whether your current pricing structure is still optimized for your mix."

Statement reader
How to read a merchant processing statement — click any line to understand it. This is what your clients see every month.
Acme Insurance Co. — Processing Statement
May 2025 · MID: 4821-XXXX · Processed by JPMorgan Merchant Services
Total processing volume
This is your client's total card transaction volume for the month. At $4.2M, this is a meaningful Middle Market account. Watch this number month-over-month — significant drops could indicate a client moving volume to a competitor or a processing issue.
Interchange fees
This is the largest line item on most statements — money that flows from JPMorgan to the cardholder's issuing bank. The "blended" rate is the weighted average across all card types processed. At 1.72%, this client is paying $72,240/month just in interchange. If you can optimize even 0.10%, that's $4,200/month savings — a powerful client conversation.
Assessment fees — check for avoidable charges
Assessment fees are charged by the card networks (not JPMorgan). Some assessments are unavoidable; others can be reduced. This is a Payment Explorer focus area. If you see assessment fees higher than expected, dig into whether the client is triggering fees through transaction routing or data submission gaps. Andrew described exactly this type of finding as an "avoidable fee."
Processing / service fee
This is JPMorgan's revenue from this client — the per-transaction fee negotiated in the contract. This is what re-contracting conversations center on. When a client pushes back on this fee, your job is to demonstrate the value delivered — auth rate optimization, proactive monitoring, QBR insights — that justifies the relationship cost.
Chargeback fees — watch the ratio
18 chargebacks on 42,000 transactions = 0.043% ratio — well within Visa's 1% threshold. But each chargeback costs $25 in fees plus the lost transaction value. If this number starts climbing, it's a conversation to have before it becomes a network monitoring issue. Andrew's card testing scenario — where chargebacks spike suddenly — would show up dramatically in this line.
PCI compliance fee
This is a flat monthly fee for JPMorgan's PCI compliance program — it covers the client's annual SAQ (Self-Assessment Questionnaire) and breach insurance. If a client is non-compliant, this fee is often replaced by a much larger non-compliance fee ($100+/month). When you see PCI non-compliance fees on a statement, that's an urgent conversation.

How the three relationships interact
RM → Banker
Keep banker updated on merchant services health and opportunities. A client growing payment volume = more revenue for the bank overall. Alert banker when client expresses frustration — they need to know if the relationship is at risk.
RM → Treasury rep
Treasury rep often has the closest day-to-day relationship with the client's finance team. They can warm introductions, flag upcoming client needs, and co-coordinate QBRs. Share Payment Explorer insights that may affect the client's cash flow conversation.
Banker → RM
Banker may flag that a client is unhappy, considering switching banks, or expanding into a new business line. Each of these is a signal for the RM — an unhappy client needs proactive outreach; a new business line may need new payment infrastructure.
All three → Client
The client's treasurer deals with all three of you. When you're all aligned — same message, no surprises — the client feels like they have one integrated JPMorgan team, not three separate vendors. That cohesion is a retention advantage.

Why insurance is different from retail
Recurring billing model: Insurance clients process the same customers repeatedly — monthly or annual premium payments. This creates stable, predictable volume but also means auth rate failures directly translate to policy lapses, which is a customer experience crisis for the insurer.

CNP-dominant: Nearly all insurance payment collection happens card-not-present — online portals, auto-pay, phone payments. This means higher interchange rates and greater emphasis on fraud management and auth rate optimization.

Cost pressure is acute: Andrew described it directly — inflation, litigation costs, tariff impacts on auto parts. The insurance sector is seeing margin compression they haven't experienced before. This makes every basis point of interchange optimization meaningful.

Low chargeback exposure: Unlike retail, insurance clients tend to have low chargeback rates — customers generally want their coverage. Fraud risk is moderate but manageable.

Salesforce workflow
How RMs use Salesforce for opportunity tracking — Andrew said it's a mandate, not optional
Andrew said: "When you have an opportunity, you need to log it in Salesforce. Not all of us do it perfectly — if something's wrong, someone will reach out to you. Just pick it back up. But it's a mandate."
What gets logged in Salesforce
In payments RM, Salesforce tracks revenue opportunities — situations where you have identified a potential to grow revenue with an existing client or win new business. Think of it as your formal pipeline.
When to log: Any time you identify a concrete revenue opportunity — a client conversation about expanding services, a cross-sell from your banker, a client exploring a new payment channel, a re-contracting conversation starting.
Opportunity pipeline stages
1
Identify / Qualify
You've spotted a potential opportunity — a client mentions they're processing payments through a competitor for one product line, or a banker flags that a client is expanding into e-commerce. Log it now. Probability: 10-20%.
2
Discovery / Needs analysis
You've had a conversation with the client about the opportunity. You understand their current situation and what they'd need. You're building the business case. Probability: 30-40%.
3
Proposal
You've presented a formal solution or pricing to the client. They're evaluating. This is a real, active opportunity. Probability: 50-60%.
4
Negotiation / Commit
Client has signaled intent to move forward. You're working through final terms. Probability: 70-85%.
✓
Closed Won / Closed Lost
Contract signed (Won) or client went another direction (Lost). Log the outcome and reason — this data informs team strategy and your own coaching.
What to log when you log an opportunity
Required fields
✦ Client name and account
✦ Opportunity type (new product, expansion, re-contracting)
✦ Estimated annual revenue value
✦ Current stage
✦ Expected close date
✦ Notes on what triggered the opportunity
Good practice
✦ Log the same day you identify it — memory fades
✦ Update stage after every meaningful client conversation
✦ Note who the decision-maker is on the client side
✦ Flag if banker or treasury rep is involved — helps with internal alignment reporting
✦ Don't let opportunities sit at the same stage for more than 30 days without an update
The open items log vs. Salesforce — what goes where
Open items Excel: Everything — service issues, report requests, follow-ups, client questions, action items. This is your personal client management system.

Salesforce: Formal revenue opportunities only — things that will generate income for JPMorgan if they close. Andrew described keeping a revenue opportunity tab in his client Excel as a "selfishly" personal tracker — Salesforce is the official record.
